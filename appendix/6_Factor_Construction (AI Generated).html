Constructing the AI factor

Two independent constructions were built and compared side by side, rather than committing to one:

  • AI_BASKET — an equal-weight, ex-universe basket of 10 names, excess of the market return. Started as 5 AI hardware/foundry names (AVGO, TSM, AMD, SMCI, ASML), then was deliberately broadened with 5 mega-cap AI software/platform names — GOOGL (DeepMind/Gemini/TPUs), META (Llama, heavy AI capex), AMZN (AWS Bedrock/Trainium), CRM (Agentforce/Einstein), NOW (AI Platform/Now Assist) — after the hardware-only version was flagged as too narrow a read on "AI." AI-native software names (PLTR, SNOW) were considered and deliberately excluded: both IPO'd 2020+, and since the basket requires every name to have data on every day, including either would have pushed the whole basket's usable history from 2009 to ~2020, cutting the sample by more than a decade. Chosen over a single ETF so the factor's ingredients are known and inspectable rather than opaque fund holdings.
  • AI_ARTY — the iShares Future AI & Tech ETF (ARTY, formerly IRBO), excess of the market return. A real, third-party-constructed thematic instrument, kept alongside AI_BASKET as an independent check rather than a replacement for it.

Both are deliberately price/holdings-based, not text-derived — this keeps the factor independent of the text-based AI Sentiment Signal built elsewhere in this project, so the two can later be compared without circularity.

Stripping out universe names

Every name in AI_BASKET is explicitly chosen from outside our 9-company universe — none of ADBE, CAT, DELL, INTC, MU, NVDA, PG, TEAM, or WM appear in the basket. This was a deliberate decision: if a universe company were itself a basket ingredient, its own AI beta would be partly mechanical (regressing a stock against a factor that contains that same stock) rather than a genuine external read on AI exposure. AI_ARTY does not have this guarantee — it holds NVDA (~4.9%) and MU (~4.6%) as real fund positions — which is exactly why AI_BASKET is retained as the cleaner comparison for those two names rather than relying on AI_ARTY alone.

Sector cleaning — a known trade-off

Each company's AI factor is orthogonalized against its own sector benchmark before its beta is read (software names against IGV, semiconductors/hardware against a custom ex-universe tech basket, industrials against XLI, staples against XLP) — so a company's AI beta isn't just its sector beta in disguise. This was a deliberate choice over using one shared sector control for every company, since stripping an irrelevant sector (e.g. software) out of the AI factor before testing an industrial name would only discard signal for no reason.

The trade-off, acknowledged here rather than glossed over: because the orthogonalization is re-run per company, the "AI factor" used isn't literally identical across all 9 regressions — each company sees a slightly different, company-specific residual of the same underlying factor. This is the standard way to do this kind of per-company control, but it does mean the betas above are loadings on 9 related, not 9 numerically identical, versions of the AI factor.
